# Classic Stages of Writing Treatments

The traditional path from a raw idea to a locked treatment.  
Use this as a roadmap for any project in doaashow (movie, TV, game, podcast, theatre, etc.).

> Entertainment purposes only — just for goofing off and having fun.  
> Built by **Matt Grosso** ([@callmecoffee3](https://github.com/callmecoffee3))

---

## Overview of the Stages

| Stage | Name | What you produce | Typical length |
|-------|------|------------------|----------------|
| 1 | Idea / Concept | Raw spark or one-liner | 1 sentence – 1 paragraph |
| 2 | Logline | Single-sentence pitch | 1–2 sentences |
| 3 | Synopsis | Short story summary | ½ – 1 page |
| 4 | Short Treatment | Narrative overview | 1 – 3 pages |
| 5 | Beat Sheet / Index Cards | Structured beats | 15 – 40 cards / beats |
| 6 | Full Treatment | Detailed narrative treatment | 5 – 15+ pages |
| 7 | Revision Passes | Improved versions | Same as above |
| 8 | Locked Treatment | Final approved version | Ready for script / production |

You do not have to do every stage for every project. Skip or combine as needed — this is a playground.

---

## Stage 1 — Idea / Concept

**Goal:** Capture the spark before it disappears.

- Write the raw idea in `ideas.md` or a new file.
- No rules. Messy is fine.
- Ask: What is the cool part? What is the feeling?

**Output:** A short note or paragraph.

---

## Stage 2 — Logline

**Goal:** Distill the whole story into one or two strong sentences.

A good logline usually includes:
- Protagonist
- Goal / want
- Main obstacle or conflict
- Stakes or unique hook

**Example shape:**  
*When [something happens], a [protagonist] must [goal] or else [stakes].*

**Output:** 1–2 sentences. Put it at the top of your treatment files.

---

## Stage 3 — Synopsis

**Goal:** Tell the whole story briefly in prose.

- Beginning, middle, end.
- Main characters and key turns.
- No dialogue, no camera directions — pure story.

**Output:** ½ – 1 page.

---

## Stage 4 — Short Treatment

**Goal:** Expand the synopsis into a readable narrative overview.

- Still prose (reads like a short story).
- Includes major sequences and emotional turns.
- Introduces tone and world a little more.

**Use:** `treatment-template.md`

**Output:** 1 – 3 pages.

---

## Stage 5 — Beat Sheet / Index Cards

**Goal:** Break the story into ordered dramatic beats.

- One card = one scene or sequence.
- Focus on what happens and why it matters.
- Easy to rearrange.

**Use:** `index-card-treatment.md`

**Classic act structure reminder:**
- Act 1 — Setup & inciting incident
- Act 2A — Rising action / complications
- Midpoint — Major turn or revelation
- Act 2B — Stakes rise / crisis
- Act 3 — Climax & resolution

**Output:** 15 – 40 beats (adjust for format).

---

## Stage 6 — Full Treatment

**Goal:** Write the detailed narrative version of the story.

- Longer prose treatment.
- Clear act/sequence structure.
- Character motivations visible.
- Tone and major set pieces described.
- Still usually no full dialogue (or only key lines).

**Output:** 5 – 15+ pages depending on project size.

---

## Stage 7 — Revision Passes

**Goal:** Make it stronger.

Common passes:
1. **Structure pass** — Does the spine work? Any missing turns?
2. **Character pass** — Are wants, needs, and arcs clear?
3. **Tone & voice pass** — Does it feel like the project?
4. **Clarity pass** — Can someone else understand it cold?
5. **Cut pass** — Remove anything that doesn’t earn its place.

Use the **writer-area treatment** (`writer-area-treatment.md`) for notes, experiments, and open questions during revisions.

---

## Stage 8 — Locked Treatment

**Goal:** Freeze the version that guides the next phase.

- Mark it clearly as “LOCKED” with a date.
- This becomes the reference for:
  - Script / screenplay
  - Scene breakdowns
  - Crew & talent assignments
  - Production planning

After lock, big story changes should be deliberate and versioned.

## Tips for the Playground

- You can jump between stages. Nothing is linear forever.
- Index cards are great when you feel stuck on order.
- The writer-area file is for messy thinking — keep it private if you want.
- When a stage feels “good enough,” move forward. Perfect is the enemy of fun.
- Once the treatment is locked, start assigning crew from the `crew-*.md` lists and fill the contact treatment.
